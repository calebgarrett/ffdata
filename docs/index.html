Breakouts — the undrafted players who change seasons

What this tracks. Every year 3-4 players nobody drafted win leagues, and they share one trait: their role shows up in the snap and target counts one to two weeks before their price shows up in projections and waiver bids. So this panel ignores fantasy points and watches four facts about every unrostered player: last week's share of his team's snaps, targets and air yards (or RB touches) from the verified usage pull; whether next week's Kalshi or DraftKings line is above or below the Sleeper projection; where the preseason rankings had him; and how many Sleeper managers added him in the last 48 hours (the crowd).
The tiers. A — usage says starter and the market already agrees: this is the one. B — usage says starter, the market has not caught up: the cheap window, but one game is one game. C — the crowd is chasing a box score the snaps do not back: let them. W — rising, or the market prices him under the projection: watch.
When to move. The Action column is the call. ADD names the player to drop and the mechanics (HH: free agents are immediate, 1 of 7 weekly adds; BSB: free agent until his game locks, then a Tuesday-night waiver claim). WAIT means re-check after the next usage pull, Monday night or Tuesday, before BSB waivers run Wednesday. NONE means no move whatever the numbers say, with the reason. Nothing here is ever proposed over a registry hold or a handcuff.
