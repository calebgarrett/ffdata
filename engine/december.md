# The December market — strategic edges, priced 2026-09-15

Books have posted look-ahead spreads and totals for **all 136 remaining games through week 18**
(DraftKings via ESPN's core API, verified as genuine market prices: named provider, real juice,
recorded open-vs-current movement, live bet-slip deep links). Weeks 15, 16 and 17 — the fantasy
playoff weeks in both leagues — have **all 32 teams and no byes**.

That means the implied points total for every team in the fantasy playoffs is a price that exists
**right now, in September**. Essentially nobody in a 12-team home league is looking at it, and the
preseason strength-of-schedule articles that circulate instead are built on last year's win-loss
records rather than money.

**What this is not.** Look-ahead lines are posted at low limits and move freely. They are genuine
prices but a weaker signal than a week-of line. Treat the **ordering** as informative and the
absolute numbers as soft. Never let a one-point gap here decide anything.

**Where it legitimately applies.** Environment is for streaming slots (DEF, K — where the matchup
*is* the whole signal), for tie-breaks between similar marginal players, and for speculative adds
with no established role. It is **never** a haircut on an established starter's mean — a season
projection already contains the team context, and counting it twice is the error that produced the
"sell Drake London" call.

## 1. The inversion: the playoff-schedule edge pays in Heritage House, not BSB

This is the headline, and it runs against intuition.

- **BSB: 11th of 12, −135 to the sixth and final playoff seed.** The binding constraint is not what
  happens in December, it is whether December is ever reached. Weeks 10–14 — the qualifying run —
  are worth more than weeks 15–17 for this roster, because the probability of reaching the payoff
  window is what everything else multiplies by.
- **Heritage House: 8 of 10 teams make the playoffs.** Tecmo Bowlers is close to certain to be in.
  The December edge there is worth its full face value.

So the league Caleb describes as "under control" is the one where playoff-schedule planning actually
pays, and the priority league is the one where it should be discounted. Buying a December thesis in
BSB right now is a lottery ticket on a scenario that first requires fixing the qualifying window.

## 2. The IDP environment sign was backwards — fixed

Heritage House starts **six IDP**, and the offense-environment metric runs the **opposite direction**
for them. A tackle requires an opposing play. A bad own-offense means *more* opponent snaps, not
fewer. The right number for a defender is the **opponent's** implied total.

Applying the offense metric to IDP had labelled Carson Schwesinger the worst December asset on the
roster. He is Cleveland — 32nd of 32 by own offense, and **6th of 32 by the metric that actually
governs his scoring.**

The genuine December concerns in HH are **DeJean (30th) and Watt (25th)** — both on good defenses
whose opponents stop scoring in December. Neither is a sell today; both are the names to revisit in

Nothing on the HH wire beats a current IDP starter even on the December number (best available:

## 3. Week 11 in BSB is streamable — confirmed, not assumed

Six of Caleb's players are on bye in week 11 (ATL, CLE, GB, LAR, NE, SEA all off): Stafford, Lloyd,
Smith-Njigba, London, Jacobs, Myers — including **his only QB and his only kicker**.

Checked against the actual pool rather than assumed:

- **37 free-agent QBs are not on bye in week 11.** Best: Bryce Young (CAR, 308.6 season, 21.00
  implied that week), Cam Ward (TEN, 294.9, 21.00), Geno Smith (NYJ, 293.4).
- **15 free-agent kickers are not on bye.** Best: Wil Lutz (DEN, 156.2, 24.50 implied), Tyler Loop
  (BAL, 146.6, 25.50), Will Reichard (MIN, 148.2, 21.50).

So the standing doctrine holds — **do not carry a spot for eight weeks to insure a streamable hole.**
Week 11 is a calendar item for week 10, not a roster item now. It will be a weak lineup and probably
a loss, but a legal one, and it costs two free acquisitions rather than two months of bench capital.

## 5. What I looked at and did NOT find — two clean negatives

**The manager-level trade signal is too tight to trade on.** Averaging each BSB roster's skill
players over the playoff window gives a range of **21.99 to 23.91 points per game across all twelve
teams** — under two points, which is inside the noise of look-ahead lines. There is no manager whose
December is bad enough to make them a natural seller of December value. Any trade pitch built on
this would be reading noise.

**Season futures are not a signal yet, one week in.** Kalshi's per-team win-total markets
(`KXNFLWINS`) are deep and liquid — five- and six-figure contract volumes at 1–4¢ spreads — and the
biggest moves since 1 September are real prices:

| Upgraded | Move | | Downgraded | Move |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ARI 4+ wins | +18.5 | | LAR 12+ wins | −20.0 |
| SF 11+ wins | +17.5 | | LAC 10+ wins | −20.0 |
| NYG 8+ wins | +15.5 | | DAL 10+ wins | −16.0 |
| BUF 11+ wins | +14.0 | | TEN 7+ wins | −13.0 |
| CHI 10+ wins | +13.0 | | MIA 5+ wins | −11.0 |

But every one of those maps one-to-one onto a single week 1 result, and the two largest are
head-to-head pairs (ARI beat LAC; SF beat LAR). That is the market pricing one game, not re-rating a
roster. Win totals also load on defense and game script as much as offense, so they are the wrong
instrument for a fantasy question — and fantasy consensus re-rates *faster* than season futures, not
slower, so there is no consensus gap to exploit.

The one name where the move, the margin and the offensive explanation all point the same way is
**Chicago** (59 points scored, +13 on a 74,919-contract market at a 1¢ spread). Caleb holds Rome
Odunze in HH; this is a mild reason to keep holding him, and nothing more.

**Re-run the same tickers weekly.** Three or four weeks of this data will separate signal from a
single box score, and the candlestick endpoint makes the pull cheap and reproducible. Acting on it
today would be acting on one game.

## 6. Open question I could not answer

BSB takes six of twelve, with **division winners advancing but seeded by overall standings** across
three divisions. That means a weak division is a path to January that has nothing to do with overall
record — and at 11th overall, a path that does not require out-scoring eight teams is worth a great
deal. I could not read the division alignment or the current standings without a browser session.
**Worth checking: which division is Caleb in, and what is the record of its current leader?** If that
leader is beatable, the whole strategy changes from "climb the overall table" to "win three or four
head-to-heads that matter."
